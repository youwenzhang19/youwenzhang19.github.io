# Handout: Cartwright — Do the Laws of Physics State the Facts?
### Lecture based on Nancy Cartwright, *How the Laws of Physics Lie*, Ch. 3 (Chinese tr. 贺天平, book pp. 38–51)

**Text for this lecture:** begin at **§3.0 引言 / Introduction** — chapter title *物理学定律陈述了事实吗？* (“Do the Laws of Physics State the Facts?”).  
Assigned PDF = Chinese translation of Essay/Ch. 3; page range **pp. 38–51**, §§3.0–3.6.

**Aim of the lecture.** To reconstruct Cartwright’s dilemma: **facticity** (laws state what objects actually do) vs **explanatory power** (laws figure in composition-of-causes explanations). Fundamental laws “lie” if read as true descriptions of real behaviour.

**House style on this handout.**  
- **CLAIM** = thesis to remember  
- **ARGUMENT** = numbered premises → conclusion (syllogistic skeleton)  
- **EXAMPLE** = what the text uses, and *what it is for*  
- **UPSHOT** = what we are licensed to conclude next  

---

## 0. Target: the facticity view (background)

**CLAIM 0.** The **facticity view of laws** (name from John Perry): laws of nature describe *facts about reality*; a law is correct iff the world is (or is relevantly like) as the law says.

**ARGUMENT 0.**  
(P1) Scientific realists typically hold that laws describe what real concrete objects (quarks, mice, genes…) do / must do.  
(P2) Fundamental explanatory laws of physics are treated as paradigms that other sciences should imitate.  
(C) So if *those* laws fail facticity, the default realist picture of laws is in trouble—not just a local quarrel about biology.

**EXAMPLE 0 (biology as foil — Smart).**  
J. J. C. Smart: biology (like engineering) has no “true” exceptionless laws about complex systems (semiconductors, organisms). Many conclude biology is second-rate.  
**What it is for:** set up Cartwright’s retort—if exceptionlessness / facticity is the standard, **physics fails it even more dramatically**.

**UPSHOT 0.** Today’s target is not “unobservables are dubious” (van Fraassen) and not “nothing represents reality” (Putnam’s internal realism). Cartwright grants ordinary factual talk; she denies that **fundamental explanatory laws** correctly represent what objects do.

---

## I. Master dilemma: truth vs explanatory power (§3.0–3.1)

### I.A Opening thesis

**CLAIM 1.** Physics’ fundamental explanatory laws (Maxwell, Schrödinger, GR, …), read as descriptions of reality, are **false**; made true (e.g. by *ceteris paribus* clauses), they **lose** their basic explanatory power.

**ARGUMENT 1.**  
(P1) Facticity requires laws to state what objects actually do.  
(P2) Composition-of-causes explanation requires the same law-form to apply *inside* interactions as *alone*.  
(P3) What happens alone ≠ what happens in combination.  
(C) Hence a **trade-off**: facticity *or* explanatory role of the simple laws—not both.

**EXAMPLE 1 (Stanford vertebrate sketch / gymnotoid).**  
Biology textbooks *do* describe what objects in their domain do (e.g. gymnotoid behaviour). Fundamental physics laws do **not**—if forced into that mould, they are wrong *even by the lights of the theories that employ them*.  
**What it is for:** contrast fact-stating lower-level / phenomenological talk with non-factical fundamental laws.

---

### I.B Gravitation as “greatest generalization” that still lies (§3.1)

**CLAIM 2.** Newton’s law of gravitation does **not** describe the behaviour of bodies that also feel other forces (e.g. Coulomb forces).

**ARGUMENT 2.**  
(P1) \(F = Gmm'/r^{2}\) is offered as the interaction between two masses.  
(P2) Many bodies are charged; the actual force is the **resultant** of gravity + electricity (etc.).  
(P3) In cases like electron–proton, Coulomb dwarfs gravity; the real force is nothing like \(Gmm'/r^{2}\).  
(C) So, as a factual claim about what bodies do, the naked law is **false**—and not even approximately true in those cases.

**EXAMPLE 2 (Feynman’s gravity + electricity).**  
Feynman praises gravity as a supreme generalization, then notes electricity likewise yields an inverse-square force.  
**What it is for:** show, from a physicist’s own mouth, that “the force between any two bodies is \(Gmm'/r^{2}\)” is incorrect.

---

### I.C The *ceteris paribus* rescue and its cost

**CLAIM 3.** Restoring truth by a silent *ceteris paribus* clause (“if no other forces act…”) makes the law **true but explanatorily idle** for the interesting composite cases.

**ARGUMENT 3.**  
(P1) With CP: “If only gravity acts, then \(F = Gmm'/r^{2}\)” is (in-theory) true.  
(P2) A chief job of the law is to help explain forces in **complex** situations where gravity *and* other causes act.  
(P3) Once CP is added, the law is silent about those situations.  
(C) Truth is bought by **restricting** the law away from the cases we most want explained by it.

**UPSHOT I.** Failure of facticity is tied less to “physics vs biology” than to the nature of **explanation by composition of causes** (Mill’s phrase): explanatory laws keep the same form in and out of combination, so they cannot also report the actual resultant behaviour.

---

## II. Vector addition: metaphor, not a free lunch (§3.2)

**CLAIM 4.** Saying “each law produces a component force; nature adds them by vector superposition” does **not** restore literal facticity of the component laws.

**ARGUMENT 4.**  
(P1) In calculation we add numbers/vectors; nature does not literally “add” separately existing component forces.  
(P2) The force of size \(Gmm'/r^{2}\) and the Coulomb force of size \(qq'/r^{2}\) are **not** the forces that actually occur; what occurs is a resultant attributed to neither alone.  
(C) Component-force talk is **metaphorical** (or else shifts the ontology); the rewritten “force due to gravity / due to electricity” laws still fail a straightforward facticity reading.

**EXAMPLE 4 (north + east → northeast; equal opposite pulls).**  
Mill: both causes have their full effects as *parts* of the total (like table-halves). Cartwright: when a body moves *northeast*, northward motion is not a part of that event in Mill’s sense; with equal opposite pulls the body stays put—yet Mill’s picture would have it go left *and* right.  
**What it is for:** block “components exist as parts of the resultant,” preparing a cleaner counterexample in §3.4.

**CLAIM 4′ (causal powers option).**  
One can keep the laws by reading them as attributing **causal powers** (capacity to produce \(Gmm'/r^{2}\)-sized force), not successful exercise—contra Hume’s collapse of power and exercise.  
**UPSHOT of 4′.** Possible, but Cartwright doubts we yet have an account of powers that matches confirmation + prediction + explanation as well as the facticity story did. Not a comfortable resting place.

---

## III. Creary’s “influence + action” patch (§3.3)

**CLAIM 5.** Lewis Creary’s split—*laws of causal influence* (what influences do) vs *laws of causal action* (how influences combine, e.g. vector addition)—does not generally save facticity of fundamental laws.

**ARGUMENT 5.**  
(P1) Creary needs **general** action-laws to combine influences across cases.  
(P2) Outside mechanics’ vector addition, such universal action-laws are scarce; transport / irreversible processes rely on *ceteris paribus* phenomenological flux laws (Fick, Fourier, Ohm, …) and messy cross-effects (e.g. Soret).  
(P3) Onsager-style “unification” of cross-effects fails as a general, non-ad hoc method (Truesdell’s critique).  
(C) Influence-laws may name “true causes,” but describing *what happens* falls to a clutter of case-tailored phenomenological laws—Cartwright’s preferred picture of nature.

**EXAMPLE 5 (Fick + Soret; Onsagerism).**  
Fick: \(J = -D\,\partial c/\partial x\) only when concentration gradient is the sole relevant cause. Temperature + concentration → Soret cross-effect; no general cookbook replaces the missing action-law.  
**What it is for:** show Creary’s happy case (vector addition) is special, not the rule.

**CLAIM 5′ (second deficit: shadow influences).**  
Creary often redesignates the explanandum as final *motion*, positing intermediate “influences” so components can be real without three co-present forces.  
**What it is for:** Cartwright allows theoretical entities when experiments reveal causal structure; she rejects proliferating **shadow occurrences** invented only to keep influence-laws true.

**UPSHOT III.** Facticity-saving ontology of influences works locally, not as a general defence of sparse fundamental laws governing all phenomena.

---

## IV. Carbon ground state: composition without “parts” (§3.4)

**CLAIM 6.** In a real composition-of-causes explanation (carbon atom ground-state levels), the “component” results used in the explanation **do not occur** and **cannot** be parts of the actual result—so facticity fails cleanly.

**ARGUMENT 6.**  
(P1) Textbook stages (Messiah): (a) central-field → one level; (b) Coulomb correction → three levels; (c) spin–orbit → five levels.  
(P2) The explanation of the five levels *uses* the Coulomb-only three-level pattern plus spin–orbit.  
(P3) The carbon atom has the Coulomb potential yet shows **five** levels, not three; the lowest \(^{3}P\) of stage (b) matches **none** of the five in (c).  
(C) You cannot treat the stage-(b) levels as actual parts of stage-(c) levels; the Coulomb law (or Coulomb-only consequence) as a factual claim about carbon is false, while still doing explanatory work via a **counterfactual**: *if only Coulomb acted, three levels would appear*.

**EXAMPLE 6 (Messiah Fig. 3.1 — five levels).**  
Central field → Coulomb splitting → spin–orbit splitting of \(^{3}P\) into \(^{3}P_{0},^{3}P_{1},^{3}P_{2}\).  
**What it is for:** decisive case where Mill-style “parts of the effect” is unavailable; covering-law models say how *facts* explain, but here the explanatory link runs through **non-actual** levels.

**UPSHOT IV.** We rely on counterfactuals about single-cause scenarios without a model that shows how those non-actual results relate to the actual composite spectrum—pressure on Hempelian covering-law comfort.

---

## V. Composition vs covering-law / “super-laws” (§3.5)

**CLAIM 7.** Composition-of-causes is not the only explanation style; covering-law / “super-law” explanations can be facticity-friendly—but they do not replace what composition gives us.

**ARGUMENT 7 (three points).**  
(P1) **Availability:** for many proud explanations, the covering super-law is unavailable (sometimes we even lack empirical reason to think it exists)—yet we still understand how causes jointly produce the outcome.  
(P2) **Shallowness:** even when available, “all carbon atoms have five levels” (or “quail bob their heads because they all do”) can cover without explaining.  
(P3) **Complementarity:** abstract covering (symmetry groups, Hamiltonians, degeneracies—Norman) shows carbon as an instance of deep pattern; without composition we lose the concrete causal story of symmetry-breaking / splitting.  
(C) Even a complete hierarchy of unifying super-laws would still need the component laws’ joint action—and thus still face their **loss of facticity**.

**EXAMPLE 7 (quail / Norman on QM symmetries).**  
Covering without mechanism vs symmetry-based covering that *is* deep but still incomplete without compositional causal narrative.  
**What it is for:** refuse a false choice—“only covering” or “only composition.”

**UPSHOT V.** Super-laws *supplement*; they do not dissolve the facticity problem for the laws that do compositional work.

---

## VI. Conclusion (§3.6)

**CLAIM 8.** If physics’ laws are to explain how phenomena arise by composition of causes, **they cannot state the facts** (about what objects actually do in those phenomena).

**ARGUMENT 8.**  
(P1) Facticity: laws describe how physical systems behave.  
(P2) Compositional explanation requires same-form laws in isolation and in combination, with “same” prescribed results.  
(P3) Isolation results ≠ combination results.  
(C) Laws that play that explanatory role are literally false as descriptions of actual composite behaviour.

**CLAIM 8′ (reject the absurd split).**  
It is absurd to say: laws explain only single-cause cases; some nameless second device explains multi-cause cases. Explanation works the **same** way in both. So the specialness is in the **laws**, not in a second vehicle: the laws used in composition **fail facticity**.

**EXAMPLE 8 (CP-true gravity).**  
“If no charge, no nuclear force, … then \(F = Gmm'/r^{2}\)” can be (approximately) true—and then **explains almost nothing** about the charged / nuclear cases. Knowing all true laws of that sort still wouldn’t teach composite explanation; explanation would need something beyond those truths. Cartwright rejects that fork: science *does* use laws in composites—hence those laws aren’t factical.

**UPSHOT VI (course takeaway).**  
| Role | Status for fundamental explanatory laws |
|------|------------------------------------------|
| State facts about actual behaviour | Fail (they “lie”) |
| Serve composition-of-causes explanation | Succeed only by keeping non-factical form |
| Phenomenological / CP-restricted truths | Can be true, but thin for explanation |

**One-liner to remember:** *There is a trade-off between truth and explanatory power; the fundamental laws of physics lie—and that is why they can explain.*
